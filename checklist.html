DIWASS 2026/2027 Practical Checklist

A concise operational checklist for teams involved in cross-border waste shipments in the EU.

Back to EU Reg Brief
Key dates
21 May 2026: DIWASS applies to notification and movement documents under procedures involving an EU Member State.
1 January 2027: the transitional period for Annex VII ends after 31 December 2026, making digital readiness especially important for green-listed waste flows.

1. Identify the procedure you actually use

  • Separate shipments subject to prior informed consent (PIC) from shipments using Annex VII.
  • Document which waste codes, routes and counterparties fall into each workflow.
  • Confirm the competent authorities involved for dispatch, transit and destination.

2. Confirm DIWASS access and responsibilities

  • Confirm who in your organisation is responsible for DIWASS registration and daily operation.
  • Check whether your workflow uses the Commission GUI, a national system or commercial software connected to DIWASS.
  • Verify operator, site, broker, carrier and consignee information before relying on it operationally.
  • Define backup users so the process does not depend on one employee.

3. Review the data you need before each shipment

  • Waste identification and classification.
  • Quantity, packaging and planned shipment dates.
  • Producer, notifier/person arranging the shipment, carrier, consignee and facility details as applicable.
  • Transport route and countries involved.
  • Recovery/disposal operation and destination facility information.
  • Required contracts, guarantees, consents or supporting documents for the relevant procedure.

4. Prepare specifically for Annex VII digitalisation

  • Map your current Annex VII process from order creation through shipment completion.
  • Identify every person who currently enters, signs, transmits or archives Annex VII information.
  • Check how carriers and counterparties will interact with your digital process.
  • Test representative shipments before January 2027 rather than waiting for the deadline.
  • Define what happens if a counterparty is not operationally ready.

5. Build an internal control before dispatch

  • Confirm that the correct procedure has been selected.
  • Validate party and facility identifiers.
  • Check mandatory fields and supporting documentation.
  • Verify carrier information and route changes.
  • Confirm that required approvals or consents are valid for the shipment.
  • Record who completed the final compliance check.

6. Decide whether software integration is worth it

For occasional shipments, a manual interface may be sufficient. For higher volumes, multiple countries or several users, assess whether commercial software or an API-connected internal system reduces duplicate entry and operational risk.

Questions to ask providers:

  • Which DIWASS procedures are currently supported?
  • How are Annex VII, IA/IB documents and carrier changes handled?
  • Which countries and competent-authority workflows are covered?
  • Is data exchanged directly with DIWASS or through another national system?
  • How are signatures, audit trails, document retention and user permissions handled?
  • What happens when DIWASS or an external system is unavailable?
  • What are implementation, support and ongoing subscription costs?

7. A practical 30-day readiness plan

  • Week 1: inventory shipment types, countries, waste codes and current documents.
  • Week 2: confirm DIWASS access, users, counterparties and authority-specific requirements.
  • Week 3: test representative workflows and document operational gaps.
  • Week 4: fix the highest-risk gaps, train backup users and decide whether external software/support is required.

Official sources to keep bookmarked

  • European Commission — Digital Waste Shipment System (DIWASS).
  • EUR-Lex — Regulation (EU) 2024/1157 on shipments of waste.
  • Your national and regional competent authorities for waste shipments.

Important: EU Reg Brief is an independent information resource and does not provide legal advice. Requirements can depend on the waste stream, procedure, countries involved and competent authorities. Always verify operational decisions against current official sources and the authorities applicable to your shipment.
