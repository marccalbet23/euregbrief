How to Register Your Company in DIWASS

Registration is one of the first operational dependencies in DIWASS. An operator that is not registered cannot simply be typed into a notification or Annex VII document: it must exist in the system so it can be selected.

Last verified: 22 September 2026 · Based on current European Commission guidance
The first question is not “where is the registration button?”

First determine how your competent authority requires operators in your territory to access DIWASS. Some operators register through the Commission DIWASS GUI. Others must register through a national or regional system interconnected with DIWASS. The correct route depends on the competent authority and can differ between DIWASS procedures.

Who needs to be registered?

The Commission states that all economic operators involved in a waste shipment where an EU Member State is the country of dispatch, destination or transit must be registered in DIWASS. Depending on the shipment, that can include:

  • notifiers;
  • waste producers;
  • persons arranging shipments;
  • carriers;
  • consignees; and
  • waste receiving facilities.

Operators from third countries can also need registration so that they can be selected in documents involving the EU.

Registration does not equal legal authorisation. DIWASS registration is a technical identification step. It makes the operator selectable in waste-shipment documents. It does not prove that the company is authorised to act as a notifier, carrier, consignee, facility or other role. Competent authorities remain responsible for assessing whether the operator is entitled to perform the relevant activity.

Step 1 — Find your required access route

Before starting registration, confirm whether your competent authority requires your organisation to use the Commission GUI or a local interconnected system.

The Commission explains that Member State competent authorities declared their chosen access methods under Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2025/1290. Those choices were made separately for:

  • prior notification and consent, including movement documents;
  • Annex VII exchange; and
  • communication on pre-consented recovery facilities.

That separation matters. A business may be required to use one access route for notifications and another for Annex VII.

If your authority requires…Registration route
Commission DIWASS GUIRegister the economic operator through DIWASS using an authorised user and submit the request to the competent authority.
National or regional interconnected systemRegister in that local system according to the authority's instructions. The local process feeds the operator into the DIWASS ecosystem.
Commercial softwareDo not assume the software replaces registration. Direct commercial-software/API access still requires the operator to be registered and represented by an authorised DIWASS user before API details can be requested.

Step 2 — If you use the Commission GUI, become a DIWASS user

For GUI registration, the Commission's first instruction is that the person registering the operator must first become a user of DIWASS. In practice this is the human account that will submit or manage the operator registration and later represent the organisation where authorised.

Do not make this a single-person dependency. Once your organisation is operational, assign backup users and document who can approve representation requests.

Step 3 — Prepare the operator data

The registration request requires basic operator information. The Commission specifically mentions:

  • operator name;
  • main identification number;
  • address; and
  • contact details.

The exact identification number to use depends on what the relevant competent authority has declared for its jurisdiction. Before submitting, compare the operator's legal details with the authority's identification-number guidance rather than guessing which business identifier DIWASS expects.

Step 4 — Select the competent authority that should approve the request

For GUI registration, the user must select the competent authority responsible for approving the operator registration. The Commission publishes guidance identifying the authorities responsible for operator registration by location.

This becomes especially important when a company has sites in more than one Member State. The Commission's FAQ notes that, where the main operator is established in one Member State but a site is in another, the site registration should be handled by the competent authority where that site is located.

Step 5 — Submit the registration request

After entering the operator data and selecting the competent authority, submit the request in DIWASS. Registration is not necessarily instantaneous because the competent authority has an approval role.

If the authority requires supporting documents, the Commission says those documents must be provided directly to the authority outside DIWASS. Therefore a registration can remain unresolved even when the online fields have been completed correctly if the authority is waiting for separate evidence.

Practical tip

If a registration appears stalled, do not only re-check DIWASS. Confirm whether the competent authority expects permits, registry extracts, identification evidence or other documents through email, a national portal or another channel.

Step 6 — Set up representation and users

Registration creates the operator record, but users also need the appropriate representation rights to work on behalf of the operator. The Commission currently provides separate tutorials for:

  • requesting authorisation to represent an operator;
  • requesting authorisation when the user is not yet active in DIWASS;
  • approving users' authorisation requests; and
  • registering an operator site.

For a business workflow, treat this as an access-control process. Decide who can create or modify shipment documents, who can approve user access, and who acts as backup.

Step 7 — Register relevant sites where necessary

Companies operating treatment or other relevant facilities should make sure the site structure represented in DIWASS matches the actual legal and operational setup. A company record and a site/facility are not always interchangeable operational concepts.

For cross-border groups, verify which authority validates each site rather than assuming the authority responsible for the company's registered office will validate all locations.

Step 8 — Verify that the operator is actually selectable

The practical test is simple: once registration has been approved, the operator should appear as a selectable entity in the relevant DIWASS document workflow.

The Commission states that DIWASS does not allow operator details to be entered manually at the point a notification or Annex VII document is submitted. If the required operator does not appear in the selection list, the workflow can be blocked until the registration issue is resolved.

What about non-EU operators?

Third-country operators can also be registered in the Commission GUI or interconnected local systems. The competent authority chosen for approval depends on whether the relevant third-country authority actively uses DIWASS.

  • If the relevant third-country competent authority actively uses DIWASS, the Commission says the operator should select that authority for the country where it has its registered office.
  • If that third-country authority does not actively use DIWASS, the operator should select an EU competent authority that would act as dispatch, destination or transit authority for the planned notified shipment.

Registration of a non-EU operator is still primarily about identification and selectability. The Commission makes clear that registration does not necessarily mean the third-country operator must actively use DIWASS itself.

Common registration blockers

Wrong registration channelYou try to register through the Commission GUI even though your competent authority requires a local system.
Wrong identification numberThe company identifier entered does not match the identifier format declared by the relevant authority.
Wrong approving authorityThe request is routed to an authority that is not responsible for the operator or site.
Missing external evidenceThe authority requires supporting documents that must be supplied outside DIWASS.
No authorised representativeThe operator exists, but the employee who needs to work on its behalf has not been authorised.
Counterparty not registeredYour own organisation is ready, but a carrier, consignee or receiving facility still cannot be selected.

What to prepare before you start

  • legal operator name exactly as registered;
  • correct business/operator identification number for your jurisdiction;
  • registered address and contact information;
  • list of operational sites/facilities;
  • competent authority responsible for the operator and each relevant site;
  • the authority's declared DIWASS access route;
  • names of primary and backup DIWASS users;
  • supporting documentation the authority may request; and
  • a list of recurring counterparties whose DIWASS registration should also be checked.

What to do immediately after registration

Do not stop once the operator appears in DIWASS. Test the operational chain:

  1. confirm your users can represent the operator;
  2. confirm sites/facilities appear correctly;
  3. confirm recurring counterparties are selectable;
  4. check the access route for the procedure you actually use;
  5. run through a representative notification or Annex VII scenario; and
  6. document who internally owns each step.
